Wall Street Futures Rebound After AI Stock Selloff

A premarket rally offered relief after last week’s steep losses, but traders are still testing whether the AI investment boom can deliver profits.

Published November 23, 2026 · By Public Opinion News Editorial Team

Wall Street futures rose sharply on Monday after a bruising week for technology shares and companies linked to artificial intelligence. Reuters reported that Nasdaq 100 futures were up about 3.3% and S&P 500 futures 2.5% in early trading, before the US cash market opened. The bounce followed a steep decline that pushed the S&P 500 into correction territory and the Nasdaq into negative territory for the year. The AI stock selloff has raised questions about spending, valuation and profitability. A rebound in futures is encouraging, but it does not establish that investors have finished repricing the sector.

Futures offered a premarket rebound

US index futures climbed early Monday as technology shares recovered some ground. Reuters reported gains of about 3.3% for Nasdaq 100 futures and 2.5% for S&P 500 futures at an early snapshot. European stocks also moved higher, led by technology and semiconductor shares.

Futures trade before the regular market opens and can change quickly. The numbers are not the same as the cash indexes’ closing performance. The rebound provided a positive signal, but investors were waiting to see whether buying would persist after the opening bell.

The Nasdaq fell roughly 10% over the previous week, its largest weekly drop since the pandemic-driven selloff in March 2020, according to Reuters. The S&P 500 also entered correction territory, a decline of at least 10% from a recent peak. The market move spread beyond technology.

A broad decline can indicate that investors are reducing risk rather than simply rotating between individual stocks. Volatility rose sharply, and some traders sought cash and safe-haven assets. The AI stock selloff therefore became a wider market event, not just a technology-sector adjustment.

Investors are questioning the cost of AI infrastructure

Large technology companies are spending heavily on data centers, chips, networking equipment and electricity. The investment is intended to support AI services, but investors want evidence that revenue and margins will eventually justify the cost. Capacity can take years to build and monetize.

The central question is whether demand will grow quickly enough to absorb the new infrastructure. If customers use AI but pay less than expected, companies may face lower returns on capital. That concern has become more important as borrowing costs remain elevated.

Valuations leave less room for disappointment

AI-linked companies attracted high valuations as investors expected rapid growth in computing demand. When earnings forecasts, capital spending or customer financing become uncertain, prices can adjust quickly. A company may still be expanding while its stock falls if investors believe future profits are less secure.

The latest AI stock selloff shows that enthusiasm is not a substitute for cash flow. Traders are examining sales growth, operating margins, debt and the pace of new orders. Valuation changes can also affect suppliers and firms that have less direct exposure to AI.

Nvidia and chip supply remain in focus

Chipmakers and equipment suppliers have been central to the AI investment cycle. Reuters cited concerns around Nvidia-related uncertainty and new US export controls as factors adding to pressure. Semiconductor companies depend on large customers, product cycles and geopolitical rules that can change the outlook.

Investors are watching whether demand remains broad or becomes concentrated among a few hyperscalers. Export restrictions may affect which markets can buy advanced chips. Financing arrangements between suppliers and customers can also raise questions about the quality and durability of demand.

Anthropic’s debut did not calm the market

The Reuters report said Anthropic’s roughly $81 billion initial public offering did little to ease concerns about the AI trade. The stock closed lower on its first full trading day, showing that investors were willing to reassess even a major AI company amid broader market pressure.

An IPO can provide information about appetite for new shares, but it is not a complete measure of the sector. The debut’s performance may reflect pricing, liquidity, market conditions and company-specific expectations. It did not settle the question of how much AI growth is already reflected in valuations.

Asian markets showed a mixed response

Trading across Asia was uneven. Taiwan’s chip-heavy market rose as authorities moved to support sentiment, while South Korea’s KOSPI remained under pressure. Reuters reported that officials in Taiwan and China announced measures aimed at stabilizing markets and easing some financing conditions.

Government support can reduce panic and improve liquidity, but it does not directly solve concerns about corporate earnings. Investors may also view intervention differently depending on the market’s structure and the size of losses. The global response illustrates how interconnected AI-linked stocks have become.

Federal Reserve expectations and bond yields matter

Interest rates affect the value investors place on future earnings. Higher yields can make long-duration growth stocks less attractive, especially when current profits are small relative to expected future revenue. Reuters noted that markets were watching expectations for the Federal Reserve’s next move.

The week’s inflation data and central-bank commentary could influence those expectations. Investors are watching whether the AI stock selloff changes the Federal Reserve’s policy outlook. If inflation remains sticky, rate cuts may be delayed or borrowing costs could stay high. If economic data softens, investors may expect easier policy, although that could also raise concerns about growth.

Dell earnings and the Thanksgiving week calendar

Investors are looking to earnings from Dell and other companies for clues about demand for AI servers and infrastructure. Corporate guidance can show whether orders are converting into revenue and whether margins are holding. The reports will be more useful than broad promises about AI adoption.

Thanksgiving week also brings the Black Friday shopping season, a key test of US consumer spending. Retail sales and spending patterns may help investors judge the economy outside the technology sector. A strong consumer can support earnings, while weaker demand may widen concerns.

What could confirm a recovery—or another decline

A durable recovery would likely require more than a single premarket bounce. Investors will look for stable earnings forecasts, evidence that AI infrastructure is being used productively, lower volatility and improved market breadth. A rally driven only by short covering may fade if uncertainty remains.

The risks include renewed selling, tighter financial conditions, disappointing corporate guidance and policy changes. The AI stock selloff may continue to reshape valuations even if long-term demand for AI services remains strong. That is the key distinction between a market rebound and a full recovery. Markets can separate a promising technology from the price paid for its companies.

Conclusion

Monday’s futures rally offered relief after a sharp decline, but the market’s underlying questions remain. Investors need evidence that AI infrastructure spending can generate sustainable profits and that rates, regulation and demand will not undermine the forecasts. The opening of the US cash market and this week’s earnings and economic data will provide the next tests.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an AI stock selloff?

It is a broad decline in shares of companies tied to artificial intelligence, including chipmakers, cloud providers and data-center suppliers.

How much did US futures rise in early trading?

Reuters reported Nasdaq 100 futures up about 3.3% and S&P 500 futures about 2.5% at an early Monday snapshot.

Does a futures rally mean the market has recovered?

No. Futures are premarket indicators and can change. A recovery requires sustained trading and improved fundamentals.

Why are AI data centers affecting stock prices?

Investors are weighing large infrastructure costs against uncertain revenue, margins and the pace of customer adoption.

What market events are investors watching?

Dell’s earnings, US inflation data, Federal Reserve expectations and Thanksgiving consumer spending are among the near-term signals.
