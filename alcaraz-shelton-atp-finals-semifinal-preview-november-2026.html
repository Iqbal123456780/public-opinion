Alcaraz Faces Shelton in ATP Finals Semifinal After Perfect Run

Carlos Alcaraz completed an unbeaten group stage and now faces Ben Shelton for a place in the Turin final, with fitness and serve-return tactics in focus.

Published: November 21, 2026, 8:00 AM PKT · By Public Opinion News Editorial Team

Carlos Alcaraz enters Saturday's ATP Finals semifinal against Ben Shelton with an unbeaten group-stage record and renewed confidence after a fitness scare. The Spaniard defeated Flavio Cobolli 6-4, 6-1 on Friday, completing three wins from three matches and securing a place in the knockout round.

The Alcaraz-Shelton semifinal offers a contrast in styles. Alcaraz brings court coverage, variety and a strong return game, while Shelton can use his left-handed serve and aggressive first-strike tennis to shorten points. The Alcaraz-Shelton semifinal is scheduled for Turin, but Alcaraz's hamstring condition remains a key question.

Alcaraz and Shelton meet for a final spot

The Alcaraz-Shelton semifinal pairs Alcaraz, the tournament's leading contender after a perfect group campaign, with Shelton, who earned his place among the last four through a strong finish in the other group. The ATP Finals are an indoor hard-court event, where serving, timing and quick reactions can decide short rallies.

The Alcaraz-Shelton semifinal is one of the weekend's marquee matches because both players can turn a service game into a quick scoring opportunity.

Alcaraz completed a perfect group stage

Alcaraz beat Frances Tiafoe, Daniil Medvedev and Flavio Cobolli to finish 3-0. His latest win was 6-4, 6-1 over Cobolli in 65 minutes. Reuters reported that he hit 29 winners and extended his winning streak to 12 matches.

A hamstring scare remains the main uncertainty

Alcaraz said he felt increasingly confident after his win over Cobolli, and reports said an MRI scan did not show serious damage. That is encouraging, but a player can still experience discomfort during a high-intensity match. Sprinting, wide changes of direction and repeated explosive movement place stress on the hamstring.

The Alcaraz-Shelton semifinal may therefore be shaped by how the Spaniard manages the opening games. He could use longer rallies to test his opponent, but that strategy also demands movement. The medical update should not be interpreted as a guarantee that he is completely injury-free.

Shelton's route to the last four

Shelton secured a semifinal place after winning against Rafael Jodar and benefiting from results in the group. Reuters reported a 7-6(8), 7-5 victory over Jodar, a match in which Shelton had to manage difficult moments and recover after losing momentum in the second set.

Recent meetings give both players a tactical blueprint

The players have faced each other several times in 2026, including a US Open quarterfinal and the Paris Masters final. Shelton won their US Open encounter, while Alcaraz took the Paris title. Those matches show that neither player can rely on a single pattern to dominate the other.

Indoor conditions put pressure on the serve and return

Indoor hard courts usually reduce the effect of wind and make ball flight more predictable. That can help servers strike cleanly, but it also rewards returners who read the toss and position themselves well. Shelton's serve is a major weapon, so Alcaraz's return quality will be important.

The first-strike battle could decide the match

Shelton's best route is likely to be aggressive: serve wide, attack the next ball and keep Alcaraz from settling into baseline rallies. His height and left-handed delivery can create awkward angles, particularly when he targets the ad court.

Shelton will likely look for short points and use his left-handed serve to pull Alcaraz wide. The Spaniard can counter by returning deep and making the American play one more ball. If Alcaraz's movement is limited, Shelton may attack behind the serve; if the hamstring holds, Alcaraz's variety could force the American into uncomfortable positions. The tactical balance may change as the match progresses.

The Alcaraz-Shelton semifinal could become a contest of return depth: a strong first return may prevent Shelton from dictating with his forehand.

The other semifinal features Zverev and Medvedev

Alexander Zverev and Daniil Medvedev reached the other semifinal. Zverev completed an unbeaten group campaign, while Medvedev secured his place with a win over Tiafoe. The tournament therefore has a strong field despite the absence of defending champion Jannik Sinner.

A title would complete a major season milestone

Alcaraz is pursuing his first ATP Finals title. The year-end event brings together the leading players and provides a final test after a long season. A title in Turin would add to his recent run of tournament victories and reinforce his position at the top of men's tennis.

The final also tests endurance after a demanding season. Both players have had to balance travel, recovery and match preparation, and the indoor format can make points especially intense. A semifinal win would leave one more match, so the ability to recover overnight may matter as much as the tactical plan.

Fitness, momentum and pressure are all in play

The tournament's knockout format leaves little room for recovery mistakes. Alcaraz has played well but must manage his hamstring. Shelton has a powerful game and the confidence of reaching the semifinals, but he will face a player who has won three group matches.

Momentum can change quickly indoors. A strong start may give Shelton freedom to attack, while an early break for Alcaraz could allow him to vary the pace. Both players need to stay composed if the match reaches a tiebreak, where a few points can decide the set.
