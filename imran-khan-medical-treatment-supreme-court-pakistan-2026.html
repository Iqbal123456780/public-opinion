Imran Khan Medical Treatment: Lawyers Urge Supreme Court Action

Eight members of the Pakistan Bar Council have urged the judiciary to act on an earlier Supreme Court direction concerning Imran Khan’s medical care. Their statement follows an open letter from the jailed former prime minister and a government response disputing his allegations.

Published: November 18, 2026 · By Public Opinion News Editorial Team

1. Why the Pakistan Bar Council statement matters

Eight members of the Pakistan Bar Council called on the judiciary on November 17 to ensure that an earlier Supreme Court direction concerning Imran Khan’s hospital transfer is implemented. Their intervention followed a public appeal by the jailed former prime minister for medical care for himself and his wife, Bushra Bibi.

The statement gives the issue a fresh legal dimension. Rather than asking for a political concession, the signatories framed their demand around compliance with court orders, access to healthcare and due process. The Imran Khan medical treatment dispute now has a new set of legal voices asking for judicial scrutiny.

2. What the Supreme Court ordered in August

On August 18, a three-member Supreme Court bench directed the government to move Khan to Shifa International Hospital in Islamabad within two days for examination and treatment. The court also ordered a multidisciplinary medical board and allowed his personal physician and sister to remain involved in his care.

The order addressed more than a hospital visit. According to Dawn’s report on the ruling, authorities were also asked to provide his complete medical record to the court, facilitate weekly family meetings and allow telephone conversations with his sons twice a week. The Imran Khan medical treatment question therefore has a specific legal record against which compliance can be assessed.

3. What happened after the court direction

Dawn reported that the authorities took Khan to the government-run Pakistan Institute of Medical Sciences for a check-up and then returned him to prison. The transfer to Shifa International Hospital ordered by the Supreme Court did not take place at that time.

The government’s explanation, reported in earlier coverage, referred to security concerns around the hospital and the presence of supporters. The Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf rejected that account and said the court’s direction had not been followed. The Imran Khan medical treatment issue has since centred on whether the August direction was implemented in full.

4. What Imran Khan said in his open letter

In a letter shared by his lawyer on November 16, Khan asked the chief justices of the Federal Constitutional Court, Supreme Court and Islamabad High Court to secure medical treatment for him and Bushra Bibi. He described concerns about his eyesight, blood pressure, isolation and access to family and legal counsel.

Those details are allegations contained in the letter, not an independently verified medical assessment. Khan said his blood-pressure readings had risen sharply and that he feared serious consequences. He also raised concerns about Bushra Bibi’s eyesight and access to specialist care. Public Opinion News has not independently reviewed their medical records.

5. How the government has answered the allegations

Parliamentary Affairs Minister Tariq Fazal Chaudhry told Dawn that claims of denied medical facilities were contrary to the facts. He said Khan’s health was reviewed weekly by qualified specialists, that he had been taken to PIMS eight times and that doctors had described his condition as satisfactory.

The minister also said a doctor who practises in London and Pakistan had been called to examine Khan’s blood pressure. The government maintained that it would continue to provide healthcare and legal rights. Its position is that Imran Khan medical treatment has not been neglected; the underlying medical records, however, have not been published in the reporting reviewed here.

6. Why eight Bar Council members intervened

The signatories said the judiciary should take up Khan’s plea immediately and ensure that court orders are implemented. They warned that the state must protect the lives, liberty and constitutional rights of both Khan and Bushra Bibi, and reserved the right to initiate legal proceedings if necessary.

The group included former Pakistan Bar Council vice chairman Abid Shahid Zuberi and lawyers Salman Akram Raja, Shafqat Mehmood Chauhan and others. Their statement also raised concerns about access to lawyers, family visits and prison conditions. The intervention places professional legal voices alongside the political parties already debating the case.

7. Human rights organisations call for scrutiny

Dawn reported that the Human Rights Commission of Pakistan urged authorities to uphold the rights of all political prisoners, regardless of affiliation. HRCP said the allegations in Khan’s letter required urgent and independent examination, and stressed that every prisoner is entitled to humane treatment, healthcare and access to family and lawyers.

Amnesty International also expressed concern about access to adequate healthcare and regular contact with family and legal counsel. Its earlier public statements have raised broader concerns about detention conditions. For rights groups, the Imran Khan medical treatment question is also a test of whether prison healthcare safeguards apply equally to every detainee.

8. What the courts may need to clarify

The immediate legal question is whether the August direction was fully complied with and what medical arrangements are currently in place. A court reviewing the matter could examine the treatment record, the reasons for the hospital transfer not occurring as directed and whether the required family and legal access has been facilitated.

The government has the right to contest applications and present its evidence. Khan and his family can also seek judicial relief if they believe an order has not been followed. A clear, reasoned decision would help distinguish the factual medical record from political statements made by either side.

9. Why prison healthcare is a wider public issue

The case has become politically prominent because Khan is a former prime minister and a central opposition figure. Yet the underlying principle applies to every person in custody. Imprisonment does not remove a person’s entitlement to appropriate medical attention, humane treatment or a fair legal process.

A reliable system needs medical assessments that are documented, timely and accessible to the relevant court. It also needs safeguards for patient privacy. Public debate should not require the publication of sensitive medical details; it should require confidence that qualified professionals are providing care and that legal directions are followed.

10. What to watch after the latest appeal

The next meaningful development would be a public judicial proceeding or an official filing that clarifies the status of the Supreme Court’s August order. Observers should look for verifiable information about the medical board, any examination at Shifa, the submission of records and the arrangements for family contact.

For now, the central facts are that eight Bar Council members have asked the judiciary to intervene, Khan has made allegations in an open letter, and the government has rejected those allegations. The question of Imran Khan medical treatment should be judged through medical evidence and court records, not assumptions about either political camp.
