Jayson Tatum Scores 41 as Celtics Hold Off Wizards

By Public Opinion News Editorial Team · Published November 25, 2026

Jayson Tatum scored 41 points as the Boston Celtics defeated the Washington Wizards 131-116 in an NBA Cup game, ending a four-game losing streak and keeping Boston unbeaten in group play. Tatum scored 28 points before halftime, when the Celtics built an 80-49 lead. Washington rallied and cut the margin to five late in the fourth quarter, but Boston answered with a decisive closing run. The result showed both the Celtics' scoring ceiling and a late defensive lapse that nearly turned a comfortable win into a tense finish. Boston moved to 3-0 in Eastern Group C.

Tatum delivered the game's biggest scoring performance

Jayson Tatum finished with 41 points, six rebounds and eight assists in 37 minutes. He made 13 of 25 shots from the field and six of 15 attempts from three-point range. It was his highest-scoring game since returning from an Achilles injury, according to the Celtics' official recap. He also had two blocks. The line mattered because Boston needed a reliable scorer after losing four straight games. Tatum supplied early offense, created shots late and helped prevent the Wizards' comeback from reaching the final possession.

Boston built a huge first-half advantage

The Celtics opened with a 34-28 lead and then erupted for 46 points in the second quarter. They made 10 three-pointers in that period and went into halftime ahead 80-49. Jayson Tatum scored 28 of his 41 points before the break, including a stretch of three-pointers during an 11-0 run. Boston's spacing and ball movement forced Washington to defend the full width of the floor. The Celtics assisted on 30 of their 46 made field goals and shot 43.1% from three for the game.

Washington's comeback made the finish uncomfortable

The Wizards changed the pace after halftime. They scored 40 points in the third quarter while Boston managed 24, cutting the deficit to 13 before the fourth. Washington kept attacking the paint and found better rhythm from the perimeter. With 3:19 remaining, a bank shot by rookie AJ Dybantsa made the score 117-112. That five-point margin put pressure on a Celtics team that had looked comfortable for much of the night. The comeback did not erase Boston's early dominance, but it exposed how quickly a large lead can shrink.

Scheierman restored Boston's control

Baylor Scheierman had been scoreless for most of the night, but he produced eight consecutive points in the closing stretch. He hit a three-pointer with 2:31 left, added two free throws and then buried a long shot to push the lead back into double digits. The Celtics' official recap described the sequence as the key moment of the game. It gave Boston a second source of offense when Washington concentrated on Tatum. The late run helped the Celtics finish with a 14-4 stretch after the Wizards pulled within five.

Paul George and Queta supplied important support

Paul George scored 28 points, added nine assists and made six three-pointers. Neemias Queta contributed 21 points on nine-of-11 shooting, giving Boston an early inside option. Queta scored 13 points before Washington's Anthony Davis had scored, helping the Celtics establish an advantage near the rim. Derrick White added seven rebounds and five assists despite a quieter scoring night. Boston did not rely on one player alone, even though Jayson Tatum's total dominated the box score.

Davis and Washington's young players kept fighting

Anthony Davis led Washington with 33 points and 13 rebounds. Kyshawn George added 24 points, while rookie AJ Dybantsa recorded 20 points, 11 rebounds and seven assists. Trae Young finished with 17 points and 12 assists. Those contributions helped Washington turn a 31-point halftime deficit into a five-point game in the fourth quarter. The Wizards' three-game winning streak ended, but the second-half response offered a clearer picture of their offensive potential. The team still needs to avoid the slow starts that put it in such a deep hole.

The win matters in the NBA Cup standings

Boston improved to 3-0 in Eastern Group C and remained unbeaten in the in-season tournament. Washington finished group play at 2-2 and was mathematically eliminated, according to the Associated Press. The Celtics also extended their winning streak against the Wizards to 12 games. The group result gives Boston a chance to finish at the top of its section, but the final game against Atlanta still matters. The NBA Cup adds a second layer to regular-season games by making group records and point margins relevant to advancement.

Tatum's return is a major storyline for Boston

The scoring performance was notable because it came after Jayson Tatum's return from a serious Achilles injury. The Celtics have had to manage his minutes, rhythm and workload while trying to maintain their place in a competitive Eastern Conference. A 41-point game is a strong sign of offensive confidence, but one night does not settle every question about long-term recovery. The team will still need to monitor how he responds to consecutive games, travel and physical contact. For now, the game showed that he can again carry a heavy scoring load.

What Boston must improve before the next game

The Celtics cannot assume a large lead will protect them. Washington scored 40 in the third quarter, attacked the basket and forced Boston to make difficult decisions late. Better transition defense and sharper rebounding would help prevent similar swings. Boston also needs secondary scorers to stay involved when opponents send extra defenders toward Tatum. The closing contribution from Scheierman was valuable, but a team seeking a deep playoff run needs several reliable options. The next group game will test whether the Celtics can combine first-half efficiency with steadier defense.

What comes next: schedule, stakes and FAQs

Boston is scheduled to host Atlanta on Friday, while Washington is due to host Orlando on Saturday, according to the Associated Press. The Celtics can use the next game to confirm their position in Group C and build momentum after ending the losing streak. Jayson Tatum will remain central to that effort, but the team will also need its supporting cast to defend consistently.
