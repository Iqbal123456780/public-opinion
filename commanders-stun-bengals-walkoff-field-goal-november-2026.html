Commanders Win Over Bengals on Walk-Off Field Goal

Jayden Daniels found rookie Antonio Williams twice in the final seconds before Drew Stevens delivered a 26-yard winner.

Published November 24, 2026 · By Public Opinion News Editorial Team

The Washington Commanders produced one of the most dramatic finishes of the NFL season, beating the Cincinnati Bengals 22-20 on a field goal as time expired. Quarterback Jayden Daniels moved Washington 64 yards in 34 seconds with two completions to rookie receiver Antonio Williams. Kicker Drew Stevens then made a 26-yard attempt to seal the win. Cincinnati had taken a 20-19 lead on Evan McPherson's 56-yard field goal with 46 seconds remaining. The result ended the Bengals' five-game winning streak and lifted Washington to 4-5-1. The Commanders win over Bengals was built on a late offensive response, but the home team's defense had kept the game close throughout the second half.

A Last-Second Drive Turns the Game Around

The Commanders took possession with 39 seconds left and no timeouts after McPherson's 56-yard field goal put Cincinnati ahead 20-19. Daniels first found Williams for 22 yards. Washington hurried to the line, spiked the ball and used its final seconds to attack again.

The second throw made the Commanders win over Bengals possible. Williams caught a 42-yard pass and carried the ball to the Cincinnati 8-yard line. Daniels spiked the ball with five seconds remaining, leaving Stevens a short attempt. The rookie kicker made the 26-yarder as the clock expired, completing a 22-20 comeback.

Washington Improves Its Position in the NFC East

The victory moved Washington to 4-5-1 and gave the team another result to build on after a difficult stretch. Cincinnati fell to 7-3, ending a five-game winning streak. The Bengals had arrived with one of the AFC's most productive offenses, so the result offered Washington evidence that it could compete against a strong opponent.

The win does not settle the division race. Washington still needs consistent performances across the final part of the schedule, while Cincinnati remains in a strong position in the AFC North. The Commanders win over Bengals matters because it changes the mood around Washington and adds pressure to the teams ahead of it.

Jayden Daniels Delivered Under Late Pressure

Daniels had to make quick decisions with no timeouts and little room for error. His two completions to Williams moved the ball 64 yards in 34 seconds. The quarterback also contributed with his running, including a 47-yard gain earlier in the game that helped Washington set up a field goal.

The final drive showed Daniels' composure. He delivered two accurate throws and kept the offense organized. The sequence gave Washington a chance to win without relying on a defensive mistake.

Antonio Williams Became the Difference-Maker

Williams finished with nine catches for 122 yards, according to the Commanders' recap and other game reports. His two final catches were the defining plays of the night. The rookie first gained 22 yards over the middle, then broke free for a 42-yard catch-and-run that put Washington within easy field-goal range.

The performance could strengthen Williams' role. His late catches showed reliable route running and decisions after the catch, although one game does not guarantee a larger role.

Drew Stevens Made Every Field Goal Count

Stevens converted five field goals and finished the night with the most important kick of his young NFL career. His final attempt was a 26-yarder, but the earlier kicks also kept Washington close while the offense struggled to finish drives with touchdowns.

The result highlighted reliable special teams. The offense had to reach a workable distance and the protection unit had to hold. Stevens completed his part under pressure.

The Commanders Defense Kept Cincinnati From Pulling Away

Washington's defense limited the Bengals to their fewest points since Week 2, according to the team's postgame analysis. Joe Burrow still passed for 300 yards and two touchdowns, but Cincinnati's second-half offense had fewer explosive plays. The Commanders pressured Burrow and forced the Bengals to settle for shorter gains on several key downs.

The defense restricted Cincinnati's leading receivers. Higgins and Chase combined for fewer than 75 yards in the Commanders' recap. Washington did not score a touchdown after halftime, so the defense kept the team within reach.

Joe Burrow and the Bengals Lost Their Winning Streak

Burrow completed 28 of 40 passes for 300 yards and two touchdowns, according to Cincinnati's official recap. He connected with Higgins for a score and later found rookie Colbie Young for another. Cincinnati led for much of the night and appeared to have secured the game after McPherson's long field goal.

The Bengals' five-game streak ended after they failed to stop Washington's final drive. The loss does not erase their earlier run, but gives coaches film to study.

Rain and a Brief Stadium Lights Outage Added Drama

The game was played in rainy conditions, and the teams started slowly. Washington punted on its first four possessions, while Cincinnati built a lead through patient drives and a touchdown pass. Early in the third quarter, the stadium lights went out for several minutes with the Commanders in the red zone.

Stevens converted a 36-yard field goal after play resumed. Both teams returned after the brief outage. It was not the deciding factor; execution and clock management settled the finish.

What the Result Means for Washington's Next Games

Washington can use the victory as a confidence boost, but the team still needs to improve its early offensive rhythm. The first four drives produced little, and the Commanders relied on field goals for most of their scoring. Better starts would reduce the need for late comebacks and help the defense manage the game.

Cincinnati will need to respond after its streak ended. The Bengals remain 7-3. For Washington, the Commanders win over Bengals is a reminder that offense, defense and special teams must work together.

Conclusion: A Team Win With a Rookie Kicker at the Center

The Commanders' 22-20 win was decided by a final drive that demanded calm execution. Daniels found Williams twice, and Stevens completed the comeback with a 26-yard field goal as time expired. Washington's defense made the finish possible by limiting Cincinnati after halftime.

The Commanders win over Bengals gives Washington momentum, but the team still needs better early-down offense and touchdown efficiency. The Bengals will look to restart their winning form. The Commanders win over Bengals will be remembered for the final kick, yet its real value came from a full-team performance.
