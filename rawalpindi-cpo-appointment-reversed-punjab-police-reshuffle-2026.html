Rawalpindi CPO Appointment Reversed Within Hours

Punjab revised Rawalpindi’s police chief posting within hours, sending Sohaib Ashraf to Gujranwala and naming Ghias Gul instead.

Published: November 19, 2026 · By Public Opinion News Editorial Team

Punjab’s latest police reshuffle in Rawalpindi took an unexpected turn when the provincial government revised its appointment notification within hours. Ashraf, initially named City Police Officer (CPO), was redirected to Gujranwala, while Sardar Ghias Gul was assigned to Rawalpindi. The outgoing officer, Hassan Mushtaq Sukhera, moved to the Counter-Terrorism Department in Lahore. The Rawalpindi CPO appointment reversal raises practical questions about administrative continuity, public communication and how quickly a city’s police leadership can change.

1. What changed in the appointment notification

The first order named Ashraf as Rawalpindi’s new city police officer after Sukhera’s transfer. A revised notification followed within hours, replacing that arrangement. The latest order names Ghias Gul for Rawalpindi and assigns Ashraf to Gujranwala. The sequence is clear from the published reports, although the government has not publicly provided a detailed explanation for the reversal.

Dawn’s November 19 report confirmed the revised posting, while The Express Tribune reported the initial reversal. The Rawalpindi CPO appointment is an administrative decision; its effectiveness will be judged by performance rather than the speed of the notification.

2. Who is taking charge in Rawalpindi?

Sardar Ghias Gul has previously served in Rawalpindi as SP Saddar and chief traffic officer, and he has held district police leadership roles including Attock. Dawn reported that he was likely to assume charge on Thursday. His earlier experience in the city may help him understand its operational pressures.

The immediate test will be the priorities he sets after formally taking over. A posting alone does not establish how an officer will perform. The Rawalpindi CPO appointment should be assessed through public service, investigation quality, road safety and clear communication with residents.

3. What happened to the outgoing CPO?

Hassan Mushtaq Sukhera, who had been serving as Rawalpindi CPO, was transferred as DIG Operations at Punjab’s Counter-Terrorism Department in Lahore. Reporting said he had expressed a personal preference to return to Lahore. The transfer was followed by Ashraf’s initial appointment and then the revised order naming Gul.

The Express Tribune report described the changes as a swift reshuffle involving senior officers. The sequence therefore involved three officers and two city commands, not just a single replacement in one office.

4. Why did the government reverse the order so quickly?

The available reports describe the sequence but do not establish why officials changed the initial posting. It would be premature to attribute the reversal to political pressure, a disciplinary issue or a personal dispute without documentary evidence. Governments routinely revise transfer orders, but an unusually rapid correction can leave citizens and officers uncertain.

Clear public communication would help distinguish a routine administrative adjustment from a decision requiring further explanation. The government can reduce speculation by confirming the final orders and effective dates. At present, the reason for the reversal remains unconfirmed.

5. Why police leadership matters in a large city

Rawalpindi’s police command handles everyday law enforcement, traffic management, investigations, public complaints and coordination with other agencies. A city police chief does not solve every case personally, but leadership sets priorities, allocates resources and monitors the work of subordinate officers.

For residents, the Rawalpindi CPO appointment matters most when it affects access to police services, response times, investigation quality or the consistency of instructions across stations. A stable chain of command helps officers understand responsibilities and gives citizens a clear point of contact.

6. The city’s policing challenges remain unchanged

The transfer itself does not resolve the crime and safety issues facing Rawalpindi. Recent reporting has highlighted high-profile murder investigations that remain under scrutiny, alongside routine concerns about street crime, traffic and public access to police. A new chief will inherit existing investigations and operational commitments.

Continuity is important: cases should not lose momentum because senior officers change, and citizens should not have to repeat complaints simply because the command structure has been reshuffled. Investigators must preserve evidence, meet legal requirements and keep victims’ families informed through appropriate channels.

7. What should the new CPO prioritise?

Residents will look for measurable improvements rather than ceremonial announcements. Those priorities can include transparent complaint handling, supervision of investigations, road safety, protection for vulnerable citizens and consistent enforcement against organised crime. The officer can also strengthen coordination among police stations and district-level teams.

The Rawalpindi CPO appointment offers an opportunity to review how the force reports progress, but targets should be published only when the department has a realistic plan and baseline data. Regular public updates can show whether directives translate into services rather than simply adding another press statement.

8. How can the transition be kept orderly?

A formal handover should preserve records, pending investigations, operational briefings and contact channels for the public. Officers moving between Rawalpindi and Gujranwala need clarity about the date they assume responsibilities and which cases or administrative files remain with their previous teams.

Such details are routine, yet they matter after a rapid reshuffle. The Punjab government and police leadership can reduce confusion by confirming the final orders and the effective date through an official notice, while maintaining service channels during the transition.

9. What does the reversal say about public administration?

Transfer decisions are a normal part of government, but frequent or unexplained changes can create the impression that appointments are unsettled. That perception is not proof of improper influence. A transparent record of the revised notification, the officers’ responsibilities and the reason for any material correction would help the public assess the process fairly.

The Rawalpindi CPO appointment story is a reminder that administrative credibility depends on both lawful authority and clear communication. Public confidence is strengthened when the state explains decisions in plain language and does not leave residents to rely on rumours.

10. What happens next?

The immediate next step is Ghias Gul’s assumption of charge and the routine handover from Sukhera’s team. Ashraf is expected to take the Gujranwala command under the revised order. Public attention will then shift from the notification to police performance.

Readers should watch for confirmation of the new command, progress in investigations, complaint-handling arrangements and public-safety priorities. Until the government releases additional information, the reason for the quick reversal remains unconfirmed.
