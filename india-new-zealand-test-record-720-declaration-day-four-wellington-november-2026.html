India Declare 720 as Rahul and Pant Power Record Test Lead

India declared 720 for nine against New Zealand in Wellington after centuries from KL Rahul and Rishabh Pant, building a 352-run first-innings lead.

Published November 22, 2026, 8:00 AM Pakistan time · By Public Opinion News Editorial Team

India built a commanding position in the first Test against New Zealand in Wellington after declaring their first innings at 720 for nine. The total gave the visitors a lead of 352 over New Zealand's 368 and set up a difficult second-innings task for the hosts.

KL Rahul scored 136 and Rishabh Pant added 128 as India's batting order produced a huge total. The India New Zealand Test remained in progress at the latest verified score update, so the match result was not yet known.

India's declaration produced a record total

India declared at 720 for nine, their highest Test total recorded in New Zealand, according to Reuters and AFP reporting. The innings lasted 166 overs and included six batters reaching at least fifty, turning a modest overnight lead into a substantial advantage.

The declaration came after the lower order continued scoring quickly. India had already passed New Zealand's first-innings total of 368, and the final lead of 352 gave the bowling attack a large margin to work with. The India New Zealand Test now depends on how much time remains for a chase.

Rahul's 136 anchored the innings

KL Rahul made 136 from 290 balls, combining patience with selective scoring. His innings helped India recover from early wickets and kept the scoreboard moving on a pitch that offered New Zealand's seamers some assistance but also rewarded batters who settled in.

Rahul's century was a key part of India's response after New Zealand posted 368. He stayed at the crease through several partnerships and allowed teammates to attack around him. His long innings reduced pressure on the middle order and laid the foundation for the declaration. The India New Zealand Test also showed how a single anchor can allow attacking partners to play freely.

Pant marked his return with a century

Rishabh Pant scored 128 from 153 balls, including ten fours and six sixes, in an aggressive innings that accelerated India's lead. The wicketkeeper-batter's century came after a period away from Test cricket because of injury, making the performance a notable personal milestone.

Pant's approach changed the tempo. He used his reach to attack spin and pace, while also finding gaps when New Zealand spread the field. His dismissal near lunch on day four ended a major partnership, but India had already built a strong position.

Jurel and the lower order added important runs

Dhruv Jurel contributed 78, while Yashasvi Jaiswal made 85 and Devdutt Padikkal scored 80. Shubman Gill fell on 98, narrowly missing a century. Those contributions meant New Zealand could not focus on one batter or rely on a quick collapse.

Anshul Kamboj, Washington Sundar, Akash Deep and Jasprit Bumrah also helped extend the innings. India's total was built across the lineup rather than by a single partnership, giving the team flexibility over when to declare and how much time to leave for the second innings.

New Zealand's first innings was built by Nicholls and Phillips

New Zealand were dismissed for 368 after recovering from a difficult start. Henry Nicholls scored 175 and Glenn Phillips made 118, combining for a major sixth-wicket partnership that rescued the hosts and kept their first innings competitive.

The pair's resistance was important because New Zealand had lost early wickets. Their partnership changed the shape of the match and forced India to bat for a long period. The hosts still faced a deficit after India's reply, but their first innings had provided a platform.

Bumrah struck early in the second innings

Jasprit Bumrah removed New Zealand captain Tom Latham with the second ball of the hosts' second innings, according to Reuters. The early wicket gave India a chance to apply pressure immediately after the declaration and put the top order under scrutiny.

Bumrah had already taken four wickets in New Zealand's first innings. His return to Test cricket and ability to find movement made him one of India's most important bowlers in the match. The India New Zealand Test could turn quickly if India take early wickets.

The record total changes the match equation

A 352-run lead allows India to attack without needing to defend a small target. The team can set fields for wickets, use short spells from its fast bowlers and rotate the attack while New Zealand must first erase the deficit before thinking about a chase.

The declaration also leaves time for a result. India chose to stop batting rather than chase an even larger score, balancing the value of runs against the remaining overs. The decision reflects the match situation, not a guarantee of victory.

The Basin Reserve pitch has favoured batting after settling

New Zealand's first innings and India's response suggest that batters who survived the early movement could score freely later. The surface has provided periods of assistance for seamers, but long partnerships and disciplined shot selection have been rewarded.

India's total was accumulated over many sessions, showing the value of patience. New Zealand will need to bat for a substantial period to reduce the deficit, while India can use the scoreboard to set attacking fields and make the run rate less important than wickets. In the India New Zealand Test, the bowling side must balance pressure with patience.

The match still has several possible paths

New Zealand must first overcome the 319-run deficit reported by Reuters at tea. A long partnership could make the match more competitive, while early wickets would give India a chance to force a result before the final day. The pitch and weather will influence how much time is available.

The first Test is part of a two-match series, so the result also affects momentum heading into the next match. India have the advantage after declaring at 720 for nine, but the match remains unfinished and no outcome should be assumed.
