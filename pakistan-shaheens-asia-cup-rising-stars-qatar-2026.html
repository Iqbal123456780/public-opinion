Pakistan Shaheens Set to Defend Rising Stars Title in Doha

Pakistan Shaheens have travelled to Doha for the ACC Men’s Asia Cup Rising Stars, which begins on November 19. The defending champions open against the UAE on November 20 before facing India A and Nepal in a group stage that will test the depth of Pakistan’s next generation.

Published: November 18, 2026 · By Public Opinion News Editorial Team

1. Doha tournament opens as Pakistan prepare

The ACC Men’s Asia Cup Rising Stars is scheduled to run from November 19 to 29 in Doha, Qatar. The eight-team competition gives emerging national sides a chance to test themselves in a 50-over format, with matches staged at the West End Park International Cricket Stadium and the University of Doha for Science and Technology ground.

Pakistan Shaheens left Karachi for Doha on November 16, according to the Pakistan Cricket Board. Their Pakistan Shaheens Asia Cup Rising Stars campaign begins the following day after the tournament opens, so the team has a short final window to settle into conditions, complete preparations and confirm its playing combination.

2. A group containing India A, Nepal and the UAE

Pakistan are placed in Group B alongside India A, Nepal and the United Arab Emirates. Each side is scheduled to play the other three teams once, with the leading teams progressing to the semi-finals. The format gives every group match significance because one defeat can affect qualification.

The campaign begins against the UAE on November 20. The team then faces India A on November 22 and Nepal on November 24. Those fixtures create a demanding sequence, with little room to recover from a poor start or an inconsistent performance.

3. Abdul Samad leads a 15-member squad

The PCB named Abdul Samad as captain of the 15-member squad. The batting group includes Hasan Nawaz, Maaz Sadaqat, Haider Ali, Shahzaib Khan, Farhan Yousuf and Shamyl Hussain, while Rohail Nazir is listed as the wicketkeeper.

The bowling options include Mohammad Hasnain, Ahmed Daniyal, Ali Raza, Sufyan Moqim, Saad Masood, Danish Aziz and Muhammad Imran Randhawa. The squad combines players with senior domestic experience and younger cricketers who have recently featured in age-group or emerging-player competitions.

4. What the PCB says about the team’s preparation

Captain Abdul Samad told PCB Digital that the side had played scenario-based matches during the week before departure. He said the coaching staff had worked with players on their roles and that the immediate priority was to begin with a strong performance against the UAE. The Pakistan Shaheens Asia Cup Rising Stars squad will need to turn that preparation into consistent execution.

Preparation in training does not guarantee results, particularly in a short tournament where conditions, pressure and individual match-ups can change quickly. The team’s challenge will be to translate practice into disciplined batting, bowling plans and fielding under competition conditions in Doha.

5. The India A match will draw attention

The fixture against India A on November 22 is likely to attract attention because of the long-standing cricket rivalry between Pakistan and India. At the emerging-team level, however, the match is also a useful measure of how well young players handle a high-profile contest without losing focus on the tournament points at stake.

The match is scheduled at West End Park International Cricket Stadium. Pakistan will need to approach it as one game in a group campaign rather than allowing its wider significance to overshadow the UAE opener or the later meeting with Nepal.

6. Why the defending-champion label matters

Pakistan Shaheens won the previous 50-over edition in 2023, defeating India A by 128 runs in the final in Sri Lanka. The PCB also notes that the side won the previous T20 version after a final against Bangladesh A was decided by a Super Over.

Those results give the squad a recent record to build on, but a title defence is never automatic. The format, opponents and player combinations can change between editions. The current group must earn qualification through its own performances rather than rely on the achievements of earlier teams.

7. Players who could shape Pakistan’s campaign

Hasan Nawaz and Maaz Sadaqat are among the players to watch because the PCB has highlighted their recent progress into Pakistan’s senior white-ball environment. Their performances will be closely followed during the Pakistan Shaheens Asia Cup Rising Stars campaign. Haider Ali and Shamyl Hussain add further batting options, while Rohail Nazir’s wicketkeeping role can be important in a tournament where small moments influence results.

With the ball, Mohammad Hasnain brings international experience, while Ahmed Daniyal and Sufyan Moqim provide additional pace and spin choices. These are potential strengths rather than predictions about selection or performance. The final XI and batting order will depend on conditions, fitness and the coaching staff’s match plans.

8. The tournament offers a development test

The Rising Stars competition is designed to give players who are close to senior international selection more exposure to competitive cricket. A strong showing can strengthen a player’s case for future opportunities, but one tournament is only one part of a longer development pathway.

For Pakistan, the value lies in seeing how emerging players respond to pressure, adapt to different surfaces and contribute in more than one discipline. The Pakistan Shaheens Asia Cup Rising Stars tournament gives coaches a practical view of the depth available behind the senior side. Consistent decision-making across several matches may be more revealing than one standout innings.

9. What Pakistan need to do to reach the semi-finals

A successful group campaign will require a balanced approach. Batters need to build partnerships and manage the middle overs, while bowlers must take wickets without allowing the scoring rate to rise. Fielding standards and running between the wickets can become decisive when teams are closely matched.

The Pakistan Shaheens Asia Cup Rising Stars route to the semi-finals is straightforward in structure: finish among the top two sides in the group. The team should treat the UAE match as the first priority, then adjust plans for India A and Nepal based on form, pitch conditions and the points table.

10. Pakistan’s schedule and the wider opportunity

The PCB lists Pakistan’s group fixtures for November 20 against the UAE at 11am Pakistan time at the University of Doha for Science and Technology ground, November 22 against India A at 3pm and November 24 against Nepal at 3pm at West End Park International Cricket Stadium. The semi-finals are scheduled for November 26 and 27, with the final on November 29.

For supporters, the competition offers a chance to follow players who may form part of Pakistan’s future senior teams. For the squad, the immediate objective is to perform consistently and earn a place in the knockout stage. The Pakistan Shaheens Asia Cup Rising Stars campaign will be judged by the cricket played in Doha, not by expectations attached to the defending-champion tag.
