Imran Khan vs Zulfikar Ali Bhutto: Who Is the Bigger Leader?

A fact-based comparison of two defining political figures, their public appeal, governing records, ideas and influence on Pakistan’s future.

Published: November 19, 2026 · By Public Opinion News Editorial Team · Updated: November 19, 2026

Pakistan has produced few politicians who inspire loyalty as intensely as Imran Khan and Zulfikar Ali Bhutto. Both built movements around personal stories, promised national renewal and challenged established political forces. Yet they rose in different eras, spoke to different social groups and left very different records in government. Bhutto helped shape the country’s constitutional and political framework after the 1971 breakup. Khan transformed a cricketing reputation into a nationwide political movement and a lasting challenge to traditional parties. So, is Imran Khan bigger than Bhutto? The fairest answer depends on whether you measure popular appeal, political skill, governing achievements or historical influence. This comparison weighs each standard against evidence.

What does “bigger leader” actually mean?

Political greatness is not measured by crowd size alone. A serious comparison considers electoral reach, party-building, policy ideas, national institutions, leadership under pressure and the consequences of decisions made in office. It also separates personal popularity from the ability to change the state.

That distinction matters in Imran Khan vs Zulfikar Ali Bhutto. They did not govern under similar conditions: Pakistan, its media, economy, military balance and international environment changed dramatically between their eras. Any ranking that ignores those differences risks confusing nostalgia with evidence.

How Zulfikar Ali Bhutto built his political stature

Bhutto founded the Pakistan Peoples Party in 1967 and turned it into a mass political force. In the 1970 election, the PPP won the largest share of seats in what remained West Pakistan, while the Awami League won the national majority in East Pakistan.

After the 1971 breakup, Bhutto took power during a national crisis. His government helped negotiate the 1973 Constitution, hosted the 1974 Islamic Summit and made the nuclear programme a strategic priority, giving his leadership a lasting place in Pakistan’s state-building story.

How Imran Khan built a different kind of mass appeal

Khan entered politics with international fame from cricket, but his political rise took years. He founded Pakistan Tehreek-e-Insaf in 1996, gained momentum after 2011 and campaigned on anti-corruption, welfare and a promise to build a “Naya Pakistan”, despite repeated setbacks and electoral defeats.

PTI became the largest party in the 2018 National Assembly election and formed a coalition government. In 2024, PTI-backed independents won more directly elected seats than any other group, despite the party’s loss of its election symbol. That result strengthened Khan’s claim to a powerful modern following.

Economic ideas: Bhutto’s socialism and Khan’s welfare agenda

Bhutto’s programme challenged concentrated wealth through nationalisation, land reforms and stronger labour protections. It gave workers and lower-income voters a language of political inclusion, but critics argue that state takeovers weakened business confidence and failed to deliver the broad redistribution that supporters had expected.

Khan’s economic approach was more market-oriented, while his welfare message drew on the idea of a social-protection state. His government expanded the Ehsaas programme and health-insurance initiatives, but it also faced inflation, debt pressure and an IMF-supported stabilisation programme. Imran Khan vs Zulfikar Ali Bhutto therefore reflects different economic philosophies.

Political skill, party organisation and leadership style

Bhutto was a skilled parliamentary negotiator and a formidable public speaker who could build alliances across ideological lines. The 1973 Constitution emerged from cross-party agreement, although Bhutto’s government also faced serious criticism over its treatment of opponents and provincial dissent.

Khan’s strength has been his ability to mobilise supporters around a personal message and keep PTI politically relevant. Yet critics point to internal party centralisation, confrontational politics and difficulty turning a broad movement into durable institutional consensus. This is a central difference in Imran Khan vs Zulfikar Ali Bhutto.

Democracy, civil-military relations and the cost of power

Bhutto was overthrown in General Zia-ul-Haq’s 1977 coup after disputed elections and a prolonged political crisis. His government had dismissed Balochistan’s elected provincial administration and used coercive measures against opponents, complicating the democratic legacy he claimed and damaging trust among political opponents.

Khan became prime minister in 2018 with coalition partners and later lost office through a parliamentary no-confidence vote in April 2022. His attempt to dissolve the National Assembly to avoid that vote was ruled unconstitutional by the Supreme Court, a defining episode in his democratic record.

Foreign policy and the image of a strong Pakistan

Bhutto pursued a prominent role for Pakistan in the Muslim world, strengthened ties with China and signed the 1972 Simla Agreement with India. His government hosted the 1974 Islamic Summit in Lahore, while his nuclear-policy legacy shaped Pakistan’s long-term security outlook.

Khan promoted a more assertive public message about strategic autonomy, Islamophobia and relations with major powers. Supporters saw independence from external pressure; critics questioned the consistency and practical results of his diplomacy, especially as economic constraints narrowed Pakistan’s policy choices across a changing regional landscape.

What their legacies look like in 2026

Bhutto’s legacy is embedded in Pakistan’s political memory: the 1973 Constitution and the PPP. His execution in 1979 remains a national trauma for generations of Pakistanis. In 2024, the Supreme Court said his trial and appeal failed fair-trial and due-process requirements.

Khan’s legacy remains unsettled because his movement is a major political force. In November 2026, he appealed for urgent medical care; the government disputed his claims, while the Islamabad High Court directed proper care and hygienic conditions for his wife. Imran Khan vs Zulfikar Ali Bhutto remains an evolving comparison.

Final verdict: who was the bigger leader?

If “bigger” means a leader who reshaped Pakistan’s constitutional framework, left a durable ideological party and influenced the state’s direction for generations, Bhutto has the stronger historical claim. His political skill and institutional legacy are difficult to match, even for a leader with a large contemporary following.

If it means the ability to build a nationwide following, challenge established parties and dominate political debate beyond one election cycle, Khan has a powerful case. The fairest answer to Imran Khan vs Zulfikar Ali Bhutto is that Bhutto leads on historical statecraft, while Khan leads on modern mass mobilisation.

Frequently asked questions

1. Is Imran Khan more popular than Zulfikar Ali Bhutto?

There is no reliable, comparable nationwide poll measuring both leaders across their different eras. Khan has a large contemporary following, while Bhutto built a mass movement before today’s television and social-media environment. Popularity claims should therefore be treated as time-specific, not as a settled historical ranking.

2. What was Zulfikar Ali Bhutto’s biggest achievement?

His most enduring achievement was helping secure agreement on the 1973 Constitution, which established Pakistan’s parliamentary framework and remains central to its constitutional order. His government also shaped foreign policy, social reforms and the country’s nuclear programme, though those policies remain debated.

3. What was Imran Khan’s biggest achievement outside politics?

His most widely recognised achievement outside politics was captaining Pakistan to victory in the 1992 Cricket World Cup. He also led the fundraising effort that established Shaukat Khanum Memorial Cancer Hospital in Lahore, which opened in 1994 and treats many patients with financial support.

4. Did Bhutto and Imran Khan follow the same political ideology?

No. Bhutto’s politics drew heavily on socialism, economic redistribution and a populist appeal to workers and peasants. Khan’s message centred on anti-corruption, welfare, national sovereignty and a more conservative social outlook. Both used populist language, but their policy foundations differed.

5. Was Bhutto’s execution considered a fair trial?

No. In a 2024 advisory opinion, Pakistan’s Supreme Court said the trial and appeal proceedings that led to Bhutto’s conviction did not meet fair-trial and due-process requirements. The court also said its advisory jurisdiction could not undo the final conviction.

6. Can Imran Khan’s legacy eventually surpass Bhutto’s?

It is possible, but history will judge that over time. Khan’s political influence is still developing, and his legacy will depend on his movement’s future, democratic conduct, governing results and institutional impact. Imran Khan vs Zulfikar Ali Bhutto may look different to future generations.
