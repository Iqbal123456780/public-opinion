Pakistan vs Sri Lanka Second Test: Day Four Sets Up a Tight Finish

Sri Lanka lead by 82 with one wicket left as Pakistan look to finish the second Test and chase a manageable target in Rawalpindi.

Published November 20, 2026, 8:15 AM PKT · Public Opinion News Editorial Team

Pakistan enter the fourth day of the second Test against Sri Lanka with a clear advantage, but the visitors have made the match more competitive than it appeared at the close of day two. Sri Lanka reached 309 for nine in their second innings, taking an 82-run lead with Milan Rathnayake unbeaten on 60. Pakistan need one wicket before they can begin a fourth-innings chase. The Pakistan vs Sri Lanka second Test remains poised between a home victory and a final-day contest, depending on how quickly the last wicket falls and how the pitch behaves.

1. The overnight score and match situation

Sri Lanka finished day three on 309 for nine, leading Pakistan by 82 runs. Milan Rathnayake was unbeaten on 60, while Asitha Fernando had two not out. The visitors had started their second innings 153 runs behind and spent the day rebuilding after early wickets.

Pakistan took a 227-run first-innings lead after scoring 325 in reply to Sri Lanka's 98. The Pakistan vs Sri Lanka second Test looked close to an innings result before the lower order resisted. Pakistan now need to dismiss Fernando or Rathnayake to set a target.

2. How Sri Lanka fought back

Kusal Mendis made 53 and Sonal Dinusha scored 54, giving the innings stability after the top order fell. Rathnayake then protected the tail and attacked loose deliveries. The three half-centuries changed the shape of the match and kept Sri Lanka batting through most of the day.

The fightback was helped by the absence of Saqib Khan, who suffered a left hamstring strain and was ruled out. Pakistan had fewer pace options available, which increased the workload on Aamer Jamal and Mohammad Abbas. Sri Lanka's resistance was disciplined, not simply a result of Pakistan losing control.

3. Pakistan's bowling performance

Aamer Jamal took three wickets for 67, while Mohammad Abbas claimed two for 54. Razaullah also took two wickets, and Salman Ali Agha added one. The figures show Pakistan continued to create chances, even as the innings stretched beyond the expected finish.

The Pakistan vs Sri Lanka second Test now depends on how Pakistan handles the final partnership. The bowling attack may need to maintain pressure without overusing its remaining fast bowlers. The first breakthrough on day four could determine whether the chase starts before lunch or later in the session.

4. What the last wicket could mean

Sri Lanka have only one wicket remaining, but the final partnership can still add valuable runs. Rathnayake has already shown that he can score boundaries and rotate the strike. If he keeps the strike, even a modest addition could make Pakistan's chase less straightforward.

Pakistan will likely seek a short spell of attacking field placements while keeping a plan for Rathnayake's scoring areas. A quick dismissal would give the hosts a target below 100, while a longer tail-end stand could push it above that mark. No prediction is certain until the innings ends.

5. Pakistan's first innings created the advantage

Pakistan's 325 was built around contributions from Ghazi Ghori, Azan Awais, Tayyab Arif and Razaullah. Ghori's 54 helped the lower middle order, while Razaullah's attacking 42 gave the innings momentum. Sri Lanka's bowlers, led by Lahiru Kumara, prevented a much larger total.

The first innings mattered because Sri Lanka were dismissed for 98. Pakistan's 227-run lead gave them room to attack in the second innings. The Pakistan vs Sri Lanka second Test has therefore become a question of converting a strong first-innings position into a match win rather than recovering from a deficit.

6. Rawalpindi pitch and conditions

Rawalpindi's surface became easier to bat on as Sri Lanka's second innings progressed. The rearguard showed that patience and shot selection could produce runs, although Pakistan's bowlers still found breakthroughs. Day four will reveal whether the pitch continues to flatten or whether the new ball offers movement.

The venue was moved from Lahore because of worsening smog. Rawalpindi has hosted both Tests, keeping the schedule intact. Weather, visibility and the pitch's pace will influence the chase, but the available reports do not provide a confirmed forecast for every session. Fans should check the PCB's latest updates.

7. A target and a possible final-day finish

If Pakistan remove the last batter quickly, the target could be a manageable one. Their openers will still need to avoid a collapse and respect the new ball. A fourth-innings chase can be complicated by pressure, uneven bounce or a slow surface, even when the target looks small.

The Pakistan vs Sri Lanka second Test could finish on day four if the final wicket falls early and Pakistan bat with intent. If Sri Lanka extend the lead, or Pakistan lose early wickets, the match could move into day five. The two teams' approaches will matter as much as the target.

8. What the result means for the series

Pakistan are seeking a Test series win in the current World Test Championship cycle. The first match was drawn, making this final Test the decisive game. A victory would give Pakistan a series result and valuable points, while Sri Lanka could salvage a draw or attempt a late upset.

The stakes should not be confused with the broader championship standings, which depend on results across the cycle. Pakistan's immediate goal is to finish the match. Sri Lanka's task is to extend the innings, defend the target and force Pakistan to make mistakes.

9. The injury and workload factor

Saqib Khan's hamstring injury reduced Pakistan's pace resources. The team must balance the urgency of taking the final wicket with the workload of its remaining bowlers. A long final partnership could also affect how fresh Pakistan's attack is for the chase.

Pakistan have other bowling options, including spin and medium pace, but each has a different role. The captain may rotate ends, use short spells or set attacking fields. The match will test depth and decision-making, not only the front-line fast bowlers.

10. How Sri Lanka can still compete

Sri Lanka need the last wicket to add runs, then their bowlers must make the target feel larger than it is. Early wickets would bring pressure back onto Pakistan's middle order. The visitors have shown that batting for time can work, but the chase will require a different kind of discipline.

Day four: the decisive session: The first hour is likely to shape the day. Pakistan need one wicket; Sri Lanka need a few more runs. Once the innings ends, Pakistan must assess the target, pitch and remaining time before choosing an aggressive or measured approach.

The Pakistan vs Sri Lanka second Test is still Pakistan's to control, but the scoreline shows Sri Lanka have made them work. A quick finish would put the hosts close to a series victory. A prolonged tail-end stand and early chase wickets could turn the match into a tense final day.

Their first innings was short, and they cannot afford another collapse. The lower order has already demonstrated resilience, but the top order must provide a stronger start if Pakistan's target becomes more than a modest total. A draw remains possible only if Sri Lanka create enough pressure or the match loses time.

Bottom line: At the start of day four, Pakistan need one wicket and Sri Lanka lead by 82. The final partnership and Pakistan's opening response will determine the match's direction.
