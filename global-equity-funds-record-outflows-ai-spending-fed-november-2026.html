Global Equity Funds See Biggest Outflow in 14 Months

LSEG Lipper data show investors withdrew a net $22.73 billion from global equity funds in the week to November 25.

Published November 27, 2026 · By Public Opinion News Editorial Team

The latest global equity fund outflows show how quickly investors can turn cautious when doubts grow about the return on artificial-intelligence spending and the direction of interest rates. Investors withdrew a net $22.73 billion from global equity funds in the week to November 25, the largest weekly outflow in 14 months, according to LSEG Lipper data cited by Reuters on November 27. The withdrawals came after a technology-led selloff pushed global indexes to seven-month lows. Markets have since recovered part of that decline, but the fund-flow figures point to a sharp change in risk appetite.

What the latest fund-flow numbers show

Reuters reported that global equity funds lost $22.73 billion in net flows during the week to November 25. It was the largest weekly withdrawal since September 17, 2025. The figures come from LSEG Lipper, which tracks flows into and out of investment funds. They measure fund subscriptions and redemptions, not the total value of every stock sold in the market.

The global equity fund outflows will be judged by the practical steps that follow, not by the language of the announcement alone.

Why artificial-intelligence spending is under scrutiny

The immediate concern is whether companies investing heavily in data centres, chips, power and networking can generate enough revenue to justify the cost. AI infrastructure spending has supported technology shares and supplier earnings, but investors are now asking how quickly that spending will translate into paid products and lasting profits.

Federal Reserve signals added to investor caution

Reuters also linked the withdrawals to hawkish comments from Federal Reserve officials. When investors think borrowing costs may stay higher for longer, the future profits of growth companies become less valuable in today's prices. Higher rates can also raise financing costs for businesses building data centres and for households and firms that borrow to spend.

Technology shares led the sector withdrawals

Technology funds lost a net $7.57 billion in the reported week, according to Reuters. Healthcare funds recorded $3.04 billion in withdrawals and industrial funds lost $2.56 billion. The figures suggest that caution reached beyond a narrow group of AI companies, although the technology sector was under particular pressure.

Global indexes fell sharply before recovering

The MSCI All Country World Index dropped to 1,019.01 last week, its lowest level since April 8, Reuters reported. The index then recovered about 3.5% so far this week through Friday's report. A rebound after a selloff is common, but it does not prove that investors have resolved the concerns that triggered the decline.

Bond and money-market funds also saw withdrawals

Global bond funds recorded a third consecutive weekly outflow, losing $3.51 billion. Investors withdrew money from medium-term dollar bonds, euro-denominated bonds and high-yield debt. At the same time, they added $7.14 billion to government bond funds and $1.17 billion to short-term bond funds, according to Reuters' summary of LSEG data.

Emerging markets showed a different pattern

Emerging-market equity funds attracted $971 million for an eighth consecutive week, Reuters reported. That is a notable contrast with the broader global outflow. However, emerging-market bond funds lost $1.42 billion for a fifth straight week. The split shows that investors can remain interested in selected equity markets while reducing exposure to debt.

What the numbers mean for companies and workers

The global equity fund outflows show how a prolonged shift away from risk assets can affect more than share prices. Companies may face higher costs when issuing shares or debt, and startups can find it harder to raise new funding. Firms planning data-centre projects may need to show clearer customer demand and stronger returns before lenders or investors provide capital.

Workers and suppliers can also feel the impact if businesses delay projects or reduce expansion plans. Yet a short period of fund withdrawals does not automatically lead to layoffs or cancelled investments. Companies with strong balance sheets and long-term customer contracts may continue spending even when markets become more volatile.

How investors should read the outflow data

The global equity fund outflows are a weekly snapshot, not a forecast of the next market move. They show that investors withdrew money during a period of concern, but they do not identify every buyer or seller in the underlying stocks. Some investors may have sold funds while others bought individual shares, options or other assets.

Investors should compare the numbers with earnings, cash flow, valuations, debt levels and economic data. A fund-flow report can reveal changing sentiment, but it cannot replace company research or a risk plan. For long-term savers, the key questions remain diversification, time horizon and the ability to withstand losses without making rushed decisions.

What to watch as markets reopen

US markets were closed for Thanksgiving on Thursday and were due to reopen for a shortened Friday session. Investors will watch whether technology shares can sustain the rebound, whether new economic data change rate expectations and whether global equity fund outflows remain negative. The next LSEG Lipper update will help show whether the week's withdrawals were a one-off adjustment or part of a longer trend.

Another key test will be corporate guidance on AI capital spending. If companies demonstrate that new infrastructure is generating revenue and productivity gains, confidence could improve. If costs continue rising faster than sales, valuation pressure may return. The global equity fund outflows show that investors want stronger evidence before paying high prices for future growth.

Five questions readers are asking

How much money left global equity funds?

LSEG Lipper data cited by Reuters showed $22.73 billion in net withdrawals in the week to November 25.

Was this the largest weekly outflow of the year?

It was the largest since September 17, 2025, a period of about 14 months.

Which region saw the largest withdrawals?

US equity funds lost $30.57 billion, more than the reported net outflows in Asia.

Why are investors concerned about AI?

They are questioning whether revenue growth will justify the scale of spending on chips, data centres and related infrastructure.

Do fund outflows mean a crash is coming?

No. They show recent withdrawals, not a reliable prediction of future market direction.

Conclusion: The latest withdrawals show that investors are demanding stronger evidence of returns from AI infrastructure and are watching the Federal Reserve closely. The rebound in global indexes offers some relief, but the fund-flow figures underline how sensitive markets remain to earnings expectations and financing costs.
