Mullin Faces White House Criticism After DHS Setbacks

By Public Opinion News Editorial Team · Published November 25, 2026

President Donald Trump says he backs Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin, but a new report describes growing frustration inside the administration. The criticism follows technology-related disruptions that grounded aircraft, interrupted corporate email and affected banking networks. It also follows scrutiny of Mullin’s public response and a government-aircraft trip to a college football game during the disruption. The Washington Post reported that White House Chief of Staff Stephen Miller removed Mullin from television appearances. The administration has not announced a change in leadership. The dispute raises questions about crisis communication, immigration policy and the management of a department with roughly 260,000 employees.

Why Mullin is under scrutiny now

The immediate issue is not a formal dismissal or a public break with the president. It is an argument over whether the secretary has shown the command and clarity expected during a national disruption. The Washington Post reported that several administration officials questioned his handling of the technology outages and his public explanations. Those accounts relied partly on unnamed officials discussing internal deliberations. They are reported views, not an official finding by the White House. Markwayne Mullin remains in office, and Trump has publicly defended him. Criticism inside an administration does not automatically mean a leadership change is imminent.

Technology outages became a leadership test

The disruptions in mid-November affected more than one federal system. The Post reported that planes were grounded, corporate email services were interrupted and banking networks were disrupted. The scale raised national-security concerns because agencies, airlines, businesses and financial institutions depend on connected infrastructure. The White House said the administration was prioritizing safety and called for modernization funding after the nationwide ground stop. Officials said they had not identified a link to malicious actors. That statement does not establish a single technical cause. It means the public record did not support treating the incident as a confirmed cyberattack.

The Louisiana football trip intensified criticism

Mullin faced questions after flying on a government aircraft to a college football game in Louisiana with family members while the disruption was still dominating the news. The Post reported that the trip drew criticism because travelers were dealing with flight delays and uncertainty. At a congressional hearing, Mullin argued that canceling his plans as the ground stop was lifting could have sent the wrong signal about aviation safety. Critics saw the appearance as poor judgment during a crisis. Those are competing interpretations, not evidence that the trip caused the outages or violated travel rules.

The Middle East trip created a separate question

A second controversy involved a five-day trip through Jordan, Azerbaijan and the United Arab Emirates. The Post said some staff members were puzzled by the timing, while two people familiar with the planning said the trip had been arranged months earlier. DHS said the visit was intended to strengthen international security partnerships. The department also said the secretary followed travel and ethics rules, including reimbursement for personal guests on unofficial travel. The public record therefore contains two questions: whether the trip served a security purpose and whether the timing showed sound crisis leadership.

Trump's backing contrasts with White House unease

The Post reported that Trump stood by Mullin in a Tuesday interview, describing him as hardworking and saying he was doing a good job. The same report said Miller removed him from television appearances after the secretary's response to the outages. A White House official disputed the suggestion that the two men had fallen out, pointing to cooperation on narcotics enforcement and efforts against foreign scam networks. A president can support a Cabinet secretary while aides debate how that secretary should communicate. Markwayne Mullin has not been publicly told to resign, according to the reporting available.

Immigration policy is a second source of pressure

The dispute is not limited to technology or travel. The Post reported that immigration hard-liners criticized Mullin for positions they consider too flexible, including his openness to a legal-status deal for some undocumented migrants. Mullin has also said he wants enforcement to focus on people convicted of crimes, while continuing the administration's broader deportation campaign. The report said federal officers have continued arrests involving people without criminal records. The policy debate is wider than a disagreement over tone. It concerns enforcement priorities, treatment of migrants and whether businesses can retain workers they say are essential.

DHS is larger than border enforcement

The Department of Homeland Security was created in 2003 to bring together functions from 22 federal agencies. Its responsibilities include immigration enforcement, border security, aviation security, cybersecurity, disaster response, Coast Guard operations and critical-infrastructure protection. The Post described the department as a bureaucracy of about 260,000 employees. That scale makes crisis management difficult because a secretary must coordinate specialized agencies while explaining technical problems to the public. Markwayne Mullin's supporters point to his business and Senate experience. Critics question whether that background prepared him to oversee such a complex organization.

Congress wants answers on outages and detention

Democratic lawmakers have pledged investigations into the flight disruptions, immigration enforcement and contracts involving detention facilities, the Post reported. Senator Gary Peters, the top Democrat on the Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee, said the administration needed to explain what happened more clearly. The White House modernization statement provides one official response, but lawmakers may seek timelines, technical assessments and agency records. Questions about immigration arrests and detention contracts are separate oversight matters. They should not be presented as proof of wrongdoing. Markwayne Mullin may face more direct questions as hearings proceed.

Mullin's job security remains uncertain

The Post reported that some officials discussed whether removing Mullin would be feasible, but it also said Trump remained personally fond of him. The report noted a practical political constraint: confirming a successor before Democrats take control of the House in January could be difficult. That does not guarantee Mullin will remain in the job. Cabinet positions can change quickly, and the president retains broad authority to replace secretaries. For now, the evidence points to internal criticism, public presidential support and continuing policy disputes. No official announcement has confirmed a departure or replacement.

What to watch next: evidence, oversight and FAQs

The next developments will depend on whether investigators identify a technical cause for the outages, whether the administration releases a clearer timeline and whether congressional committees obtain records. The immigration debate will also continue because it involves enforcement priorities, labor needs and the treatment of people with no criminal convictions. Markwayne Mullin's standing will be easier to assess after those questions receive public answers. Until then, readers should distinguish anonymous accounts of internal frustration from formal decisions and verified findings. What caused the mid-November technology disruptions?

The public reporting reviewed here does not establish one confirmed cause. Officials said they had not identified a link to malicious actors.

Did Mullin resign?

No. The reporting describes criticism and debate, not an announced resignation.

Why was his football trip controversial?

Critics questioned the timing because travelers were dealing with a nationwide ground stop and related disruption.

What did DHS say about the Middle East trip?

DHS said the visit involved diplomatic and bilateral security meetings and that travel rules were followed.

What happens next?

Congressional oversight, technical findings and the administration's public response will shape the next stage.

Bottom line: The dispute is a test of crisis leadership as much as personal standing. The strongest conclusions should wait for technical findings, official records and any formal personnel decision.
