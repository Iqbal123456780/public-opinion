Antonelli Sets Pace in Qatar GP Practice

Kimi Antonelli led both Friday practice sessions at Lusail, but qualifying and Sunday's race still leave room for Mercedes rivals to respond.

Published November 28, 2026 · By Public Opinion News Editorial Team

The Qatar Grand Prix qualifying is the next major test after Mercedes led both Friday practice sessions at Lusail. Mercedes arrive at Saturday's qualifying with the fastest times from both Friday practice sessions, but tyre limits and disrupted running leave room for rivals to respond. The times point to a close fight behind Antonelli, but practice is not a starting grid and the tyre rules will shape the weekend.

Mercedes set the early pace in Qatar

Kimi Antonelli topped both Friday practice sessions at the Qatar Grand Prix, giving Mercedes an early advantage before Saturday's qualifying. The Italian set a 1:23.217 in the first session and improved to 1:23.077 in the second. George Russell finished second in both sessions, closing to within 0.127 seconds in FP2. The results gave Mercedes a one-two on Friday and made the team the early benchmark at Lusail.

What Friday's second practice revealed

FP2 took place under the floodlights, in conditions closer to those expected for qualifying. The session was disrupted by two red flags, which limited the time available for some drivers to complete their fastest laps. Antonelli still held the top position, while Russell and the McLaren drivers remained within a few tenths. The official order placed Lando Norris third, Oscar Piastri fourth and Max Verstappen fifth.

Why qualifying could produce a different order

Practice sessions are used to test fuel loads, tyre compounds and different car settings. Teams may not run the same programme, so a driver who finishes outside the top positions can still be competitive when the cars are lighter and engine settings change. The red flags in FP2 also reduced the number of uninterrupted qualifying simulations. The Qatar Grand Prix qualifying session will provide the first direct comparison when teams run for a grid position.

Lusail's fast corners test car balance

The Lusail International Circuit is 5.419 kilometres long and has 16 corners. Formula 1 says the track was built originally for motorcycle racing and hosted its first Grand Prix in 2021. Its flowing layout includes medium- and high-speed turns, while the main straight is more than one kilometre long and offers an overtaking opportunity into Turn 1. Drivers need aerodynamic stability and confidence through the fast sections. That layout makes Qatar Grand Prix qualifying especially demanding because one error can erase a fast lap.

A 27-lap tyre limit changes the weekend

Pirelli has set a maximum of 27 laps for each tyre set during the Qatar weekend, with laps counted cumulatively across sessions. The limit is a precaution because Lusail's high-speed corners place heavy energy and thermal loads on the tyres. Formula 1 reported that the 2026 limit is two laps higher than last year's 25-lap cap. Teams must track the mileage on every set and protect enough fresh rubber for the decisive sessions.

A two-stop race is built into the rules

The Grand Prix is scheduled for 57 laps, while each tyre set can cover no more than 27 laps. That makes at least two pit stops necessary for every driver if the race runs to its planned distance. Pirelli said the measure was agreed with the FIA and Formula 1 to prevent tyres from being used beyond safe operating limits. Teams will still choose when to stop and which compounds to use, and a safety car could change the value of an early pit stop.

McLaren and Red Bull have room to respond

Norris and Piastri were close enough to Mercedes in FP2 to remain in contention for the front rows. Red Bull's Verstappen was fifth, less than half a second behind Antonelli, while Isack Hadjar was also within a similar margin. Norris said Mercedes looked several tenths quicker during Friday's running, according to Reuters. Verstappen reported that his car did not feel settled, leaving Red Bull work to do before qualifying. The Qatar Grand Prix qualifying battle could tighten if the teams find a cleaner lap.

Ferrari needs a cleaner run

Ferrari's Friday was less straightforward. Charles Leclerc lost time after a spin, while Lewis Hamilton stopped with a power issue near the end of FP2. The team will need to understand whether those problems were isolated or part of a wider setup challenge. A clean final practice session will give Ferrari a chance to test changes before qualifying. The team must also review tyre temperatures and long-run data before deciding how aggressively to use its remaining sets.

Qualifying and race times for fans

Formula 1's official timetable lists final practice for Saturday afternoon and qualifying for 9 p.m. local time in Qatar. The Grand Prix is scheduled for Sunday, November 29, at 7 p.m. local time and will run for 57 laps, subject to race-control procedures. Qatar is three hours ahead of UTC and two hours behind Pakistan. That places qualifying at 11 p.m. Pakistan time and the race at 9 p.m. on Sunday.

What to watch when the cars return

The key questions are whether Mercedes can preserve its pace over a single lap, whether McLaren can close the gap and whether Red Bull can improve Verstappen's balance. Ferrari will want a clean session after its Friday interruptions. Teams will also watch track temperature and grip because both can change during the evening. The Qatar Grand Prix qualifying session is the next major test for a Mercedes team that has led both Friday practices, but the fight for the front rows is not over.
