Pakistan Face Australia in Azlan Shah Cup Test

Pakistan’s strong opening wins set up a tougher test against Australia, one of the tournament’s leading teams, in Ipoh.

Published November 23, 2026 · By Public Opinion News Editorial Team

Pakistan’s men’s hockey team enters its Monday fixture against Australia with two convincing victories at the 32nd Sultan Azlan Shah Cup in Ipoh, Malaysia. The Green Shirts beat Canada and the United States by 5-1 scores, while Australia also opened strongly. The Pakistan Australia hockey match offers an early measure of Pakistan’s progress against one of the sport’s established sides. It is a round-robin fixture, not the final, and the tournament still has several matches to play. The result could help shape the standings, but it will not decide the championship on its own.

Pakistan arrive with two wins

Pakistan opened the tournament with a 5-1 victory over Canada and followed it with another 5-1 win against the United States. The results gave the team a positive start and showed that it could score from more than one attacking route. The official FIH tournament feed lists both results in the group stage.

Two wins provide confidence, but the scorelines should not be treated as proof that Pakistan has already secured a place in the final. The round-robin standings will depend on the remaining fixtures. Australia, Malaysia and Pakistan have all started well, making Monday’s game important for momentum.

Australia have also started strongly

Australia defeated the United States 5-1 and South Korea 7-1 in their opening matches, according to the tournament results feed. The Kookaburras are among the most successful teams in the Sultan Azlan Shah Cup’s history and use the event as preparation for their upcoming international season.

The Pakistan Australia hockey contest therefore brings together two teams with strong early attacking numbers. Australia’s ability to spread goals among different players makes them difficult to defend against, while Pakistan will look to maintain its recent scoring form.

The tournament format keeps every match important

The 2026 event features six teams—Pakistan, Australia, Malaysia, Canada, South Korea and the United States—in a round-robin stage. Teams play each opponent once before classification matches on the final day. The top two teams are scheduled to meet in the final, with other positions decided through classification games.

That format gives teams several opportunities to recover from a defeat. It also makes goal difference and defensive performance relevant if teams finish with similar points. Pakistan’s early wins provide a platform, but the standings can change quickly as the leading sides meet.

Penalty corners could be a decisive weapon

Pakistan’s opening win over Canada featured a hat-trick from Sufyan Khan through penalty corners, according to match reporting. Set pieces can be especially valuable against a defence that limits open-play chances. Pakistan will want to earn corners through pressure and convert them at a reliable rate.

Australia’s defence will likely study Pakistan’s routines and positioning. The hosts will need variation in delivery, timing and finishing to avoid becoming predictable. A successful penalty-corner plan can change the match, but it cannot replace movement and possession in open play.

Pakistan must keep defensive discipline

Pakistan conceded one goal in each of its first two matches. Against Australia, the defensive challenge may be sharper because the opponent has shown a high scoring rate. Maintaining compact spacing, tracking runners and avoiding unnecessary turnovers will be important.

Defence also begins with the midfield. If Pakistan loses possession in dangerous areas, Australia can attack quickly and force the back line into difficult decisions. A controlled transition from attack to defence may be as important as the team’s finishing.

Australia’s pace can test the back line

The Kookaburras are known for a fast, direct style and pressure around the circle. Pakistan will need to protect the central channels and avoid allowing easy entries into the shooting area. Australia’s early tournament results suggest that it can turn sustained pressure into multiple goals.

Pakistan can respond by keeping the ball, moving it quickly and making Australia defend for longer periods. A patient build-up may reduce the number of counterattacks and create opportunities to draw defenders out of position.

The importance of midfield control

Midfield possession can determine how much time a team spends attacking or defending. Pakistan’s players will need to support the ball carrier, offer safe passing options and switch play when one side becomes crowded. The ability to maintain possession under pressure can help the team settle after a setback.

Australia will likely try to disrupt that rhythm with high pressure. The team that handles the first pass after winning the ball may gain an advantage. This tactical contest could decide which side creates more clear chances rather than simply more circle entries.

What the early results say—and do not say

Pakistan’s two 5-1 wins are encouraging, but the opponents and match situations differ from the Australia fixture. A tournament is a sequence of tests, and teams often adjust their tactics after the opening games. Australia’s two wins are also impressive, including a seven-goal result against Korea.

Neither side should assume that its early scoring will continue at the same rate. The Pakistan Australia hockey match is a better indicator of how each team responds to a stronger opponent, especially under pressure and when chances are limited.

Tournament goals and the wider rebuild

Pakistan is seeking to build momentum in international hockey after years of uneven results. Strong performances in Ipoh can help players gain confidence and improve their ranking position, but sustainable progress requires coaching continuity, fitness, competition exposure and support for domestic development.

Hosting future international tournaments could also provide opportunities for younger players and local fans. The national side’s immediate goal is to compete for the Azlan Shah Cup final, while the longer-term challenge is to establish consistent results against top-ranked teams.

What to watch in the match

Three areas are likely to matter: penalty-corner execution, defensive discipline and midfield control. Pakistan must convert set-piece opportunities, limit Australia’s fast attacks and maintain possession when the pressure rises. Australia will look to exploit space, move the ball quickly and test the goalkeeper with sustained circle pressure.

The Pakistan Australia hockey game is an important group match, but the round-robin still leaves room for both teams to recover or improve their position.

Conclusion

Pakistan’s unbeaten start creates a positive mood, but Australia presents a more demanding test. The Pakistan Australia hockey fixture will reveal how well the team’s attack, defence and midfield work against a strong opponent. A win would strengthen Pakistan’s final prospects; a loss would still leave opportunities in the remaining group matches.
