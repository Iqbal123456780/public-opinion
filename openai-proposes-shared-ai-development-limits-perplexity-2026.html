OpenAI Proposes Shared Limits for AI Development

The proposal calls for a shared measure of AI progress and limits until developers improve their ability to monitor powerful models.

Published November 23, 2026 · By Public Opinion News Editorial Team

OpenAI chief scientist Jakub Pachocki has proposed that frontier AI developers agree on shared limits for training increasingly capable models. His November 22 essay suggests using perplexity and other measures to track progress, then holding development below agreed thresholds until monitoring and safety systems reach concrete milestones. CEO Sam Altman endorsed the idea. The proposal is not an adopted industry standard, and it does not specify a first threshold, enforcement mechanism or signatories. The debate over AI development limits is growing as researchers warn that capabilities may advance faster than tools for understanding model behavior.

The proposal calls for a common industry rulebook

Pachocki argues that developers should compare progress using a shared measure and agree not to cross certain capability limits until they can better understand and monitor models. The proposed framework would define an initial limit and specify what safety milestones must be achieved before developers raise it.

The AI development limits proposal is intended to reduce pressure on individual companies to race ahead because competitors are doing so. A common agreement could create a shared expectation, but it would require participation from multiple labs and a way to verify compliance.

Perplexity measures how well a model predicts text

Perplexity is a measure of how well a language model predicts the next token in a dataset. Lower perplexity generally indicates better prediction. Pachocki argues that the metric can help track the underlying progress made during pretraining, before later fine-tuning and reasoning methods are applied.

He also published OpenAI’s internal measurements on a held-out code dataset. The data show progress across model generations, but the company’s numbers are based on its own dataset and evaluation setup. Comparisons across labs would require compatible methods.

OpenAI says capability changes can emerge unexpectedly

Pachocki said improvements in pretraining have been associated with new abilities in areas such as mathematics and cybersecurity. He cautioned that the exact capabilities that emerge at particular metric levels are difficult to predict. The proposal is therefore meant to track progress before every downstream ability is fully understood.

The essay suggests that future models could show more transformative capabilities as the metric improves. That is a forecast, not a confirmed timeline. Researchers will need to test systems across tasks and environments to determine whether the measure is useful for anticipating risks.

The first limit and safety milestone are not yet defined

The proposal does not identify a numerical threshold for the initial limit. Pachocki says developers would need to agree on a measurement and a concrete monitoring milestone, such as a successor to current chain-of-thought monitors. The details remain open for discussion.

An industry standard for AI development limits would need clear definitions, a process for updating limits and a method for assessing whether a model crosses the threshold. Without those details, the proposal is a starting point for governance rather than a rule that companies can follow today.

Monitoring models is a central challenge

AI safety researchers use evaluations, red-team tests, behavior monitoring and interpretability tools to look for dangerous capabilities or unexpected actions. Pachocki argues that these methods must improve before developers allow models to pass agreed capability limits. The challenge is to monitor systems that may be difficult to predict.

A monitor can miss behavior, produce false alarms or be manipulated by a model. Shared tests may help researchers compare results, but they must be robust, updated and independently reviewed. Safety milestones need evidence that the tools work in realistic conditions.

The metric has important limitations

Perplexity does not directly measure how well a model performs every task. The OpenAI essay notes that different labs use different datasets and data mixtures, making cross-company comparisons difficult. The metric also does not capture all improvements made through reasoning, fine-tuning or other techniques.

A model with a lower score on one dataset is not automatically safer or more capable in every domain. Any common framework would need several measurements and task-specific evaluations. Independent researchers would need access to enough information to check the claims.

AI may accelerate the research process itself

Pachocki warned that AI could contribute to advances in pretraining research as soon as 2027. If models help researchers develop better training methods, progress could accelerate. The concern is that safety and monitoring research might not move at the same pace.

That scenario remains uncertain. AI-assisted research can improve productivity, but it does not guarantee a sudden breakthrough or fully automated development. The proposal asks companies to plan for acceleration rather than assume that capability gains will be gradual.

Industry participation would be necessary for the limits to work

AI development limits would need agreement from major developers, including companies with different technical approaches and business incentives. The proposal may be difficult to adopt if firms disagree about the metric, the threshold or how much information they should disclose.

A voluntary agreement could be an early step, while governments and standards bodies may later consider formal rules. The NIST AI Risk Management Framework offers a general risk-management structure, but it is not a specific perplexity-based limit for frontier model training.

Transparency could help researchers compare progress

OpenAI’s publication of internal figures is intended to encourage transparency. If other labs publish comparable metrics, researchers may better understand how quickly pretraining is improving. Public data could also support independent analysis of the relationship between training progress and emerging capabilities.

Transparency has trade-offs. Companies may worry about revealing competitive information, while researchers need enough detail to reproduce results. A shared standard would have to define what is disclosed, how data are protected and whether external evaluators can verify measurements.

Conclusion

The proposal puts a concrete idea into the AI safety debate: AI development limits, common capability measurements and safety milestones before those limits are raised. It remains a proposal, not a binding agreement. Its usefulness will depend on other developers participating, the metric’s limits being addressed and monitoring tools being tested independently.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are AI development limits?

They are proposed thresholds on model training or capability that developers would agree not to cross until safety and monitoring milestones are met.

What is perplexity?

Perplexity measures how well a language model predicts the next token in a dataset. Lower values generally mean better prediction.

Has the AI industry adopted OpenAI’s proposal?

No. It is a proposal from OpenAI’s chief scientist. No industry agreement or enforcement mechanism was announced.

Why is perplexity controversial as a shared measure?

Labs use different datasets and training methods, and the metric does not directly capture every capability or safety risk.

When could AI accelerate its own research?

Pachocki suggested it could happen as soon as 2027, but that is a forecast and remains uncertain.
