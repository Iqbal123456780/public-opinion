Pakistan Economy Outlook: SBP Forecasts 3.5% to 4.5% Growth in FY2026–27

The State Bank of Pakistan projects 3.5% to 4.5% economic growth in FY27 but warns that jobs, productivity, climate risks and structural reforms remain major challenges.

Published: November 18, 2026 · By Public Opinion News Editorial Team

Pakistan's economic outlook has received a cautiously positive forecast from the State Bank of Pakistan (SBP). In its Annual Report on the State of Pakistan's Economy for 2025–26, the central bank projected real GDP growth of 3.5% to 4.5% in FY2026–27. It also expects inflation to gradually move towards its medium-term target range of 5% to 7%.

The forecast suggests that economic activity could continue recovering, supported by domestic demand and stronger output in some sectors. But the report also makes clear that better headline numbers will not automatically solve unemployment, low wages, weak productivity or the structural problems that have limited Pakistan's long-term growth.

1. What the State Bank of Pakistan Forecast for FY27

The SBP expects Pakistan's real GDP to expand between 3.5% and 4.5% in the fiscal year 2026–27. The forecast appears in the central bank's annual assessment released in November 2026. It reflects an improving macroeconomic position, a gradual recovery in domestic demand and expected support from recent fiscal measures.

This is a forecast, not a confirmed outcome. Actual growth will depend on agricultural performance, energy prices, exports, investment, policy decisions and global conditions over the year.

2. Why Pakistan's Economic Growth Could Improve

The central bank says the recovery in commodity-producing sectors could support expansion in services. It points to higher output of key kharif crops, including sugarcane, rice and maize, compared with the previous year. Large-scale manufacturing also provided an important boost to activity in FY26.

Domestic demand is expected to recover gradually. If businesses invest, households maintain spending and production improves, the wider economy could benefit. However, the strength of that recovery will depend on whether firms can access reliable energy, finance and predictable regulations.

3. Pakistan GDP Growth Forecast: The Key Numbers

The headline growth estimate is only one part of the outlook. The SBP report also discusses inflation, the current account, reserves and the risks facing agriculture and external trade.

4. Inflation Outlook: Will Prices Become Easier for Households?

The SBP expects inflation to gradually ease towards its medium-term target range of 5% to 7% during FY27. However, it warns that a global energy price shock could place upward pressure on domestic prices. Energy costs can affect transport, electricity, production and the price of everyday goods.

For families, a lower inflation rate does not necessarily mean that prices return to previous levels. It means prices may rise more slowly. Real relief will depend on wage growth, employment opportunities and whether essential household costs become more manageable.

5. Agriculture and Climate Risks Could Change the Forecast

Agriculture remains important for Pakistan's economy, food supply and rural employment. The SBP highlights the risk that worsening El Niño conditions could affect agricultural production. At the same time, the latest information cited in the report showed improved output expectations for sugarcane, rice and maize.

Climate shocks, floods, heat and water stress can disrupt crops and supply chains. Better irrigation, crop planning, early-warning systems and climate-resilient farming can help reduce these risks. Investment in solar energy for agriculture also needs to be balanced with water use, food security and environmental protection.

6. Exports, Imports and the Current Account Position

The central bank expects economic expansion, tariff rationalisation and higher global commodity prices to increase imports. Exports are expected to recover moderately, supported by policy incentives and a slight improvement in global demand. The balance between these flows will matter for Pakistan's external stability.

The SBP projects the current account deficit to remain between zero and 1% of GDP in FY27, helped by continued workers' remittances. It also expects foreign exchange reserves to receive support from official inflows, foreign exchange purchases and financing raised through international markets.

7. The Jobs Challenge: Why Growth Must Reach Ordinary Workers

One of the report's most important warnings concerns employment. Dawn's coverage of the SBP report highlighted that unemployment rose to 7.2% in FY25, compared with 6.3% in FY21 and 5.7% in FY18. The central bank also warned about working poverty, low wages and informal employment.

Economic growth matters most when it creates stable jobs and improves household incomes. Pakistan needs more productive businesses, better skills, higher female labour-force participation and pathways for young people to enter formal employment. Growth that does not create enough decent work may leave many families behind.

8. Structural Reforms Needed for Sustainable Business Growth

The SBP report calls for reforms that address infrastructure gaps, support technology and skills development, rationalise taxes and simplify tax administration. It also stresses the importance of reducing policy uncertainty through predictable, rules-based decisions.

For businesses, stable rules can make it easier to plan investment, hire staff and expand production. Small and medium-sized enterprises also need access to finance, reliable utilities, digital tools and fair competition. These changes take time, but they are central to stronger long-term growth.

9. What the Economic Outlook Means for Investors and Businesses

A growth forecast can improve confidence, but investors should look beyond one headline number. They need to consider inflation, interest rates, currency conditions, energy costs, taxation, demand and the reliability of supply chains. Different sectors will experience the recovery in different ways.

Manufacturers may benefit if demand and input supplies improve. Agriculture-related businesses will watch weather and crop yields. Exporters will need competitive costs and access to international markets. Businesses should treat the SBP outlook as a planning scenario, not a promise of higher sales or returns.

10. Can Pakistan Turn a Better FY27 Forecast into Lasting Prosperity?

The 3.5% to 4.5% GDP growth projection is encouraging, but Pakistan's longer-term challenge is to make growth stronger, more stable and more inclusive. That requires investment, productivity, education, exports, energy reliability and reforms that help businesses create formal jobs.

The central bank's message is therefore two-sided: macroeconomic conditions have improved, but deep structural weaknesses remain. The real measure of success will be whether growth creates better employment, stronger incomes and more resilient businesses across the country.

Frequently Asked Questions About SBP's Pakistan Economic Growth Outlook for FY2026–27

1. What GDP growth range did the SBP forecast for FY2026–27?

The article reports the State Bank of Pakistan's forecast range of 3.5% to 4.5% for FY2026–27. Readers should consult the original SBP report for the assumptions, release date and any later revision.

2. What factors could support Pakistan's economic growth in FY27?

Growth could be supported by stronger agriculture and industrial activity, services, investment, exports and improved macroeconomic stability. Actual performance will depend on implementation and changing domestic and global conditions.

3. What could cause Pakistan's FY27 growth forecast to change?

Weather and crop outcomes, energy prices, financing conditions, fiscal policy, external demand and investor confidence can all affect the outlook.

4. What does the SBP inflation outlook mean for households?

An inflation forecast describes the expected pace of price increases, not a guarantee that prices will fall. Household effects depend on wages, essential goods and the actual path of inflation.

5. Why are exports and the current account important?

Exports generate foreign exchange, while the current account records trade and other cross-border income flows. Sustainable external balances can reduce pressure on reserves and financing.

6. Where can readers verify the SBP's FY27 forecast?

The State Bank of Pakistan's official monetary policy statements, reports and statistical publications are the primary sources. Check the original document and publication date.
