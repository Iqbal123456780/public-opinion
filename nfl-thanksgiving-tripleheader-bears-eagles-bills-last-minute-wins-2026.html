NFL Thanksgiving Games Deliver Three Last-Minute Wins

Chicago, Philadelphia and Buffalo all won on late scores as the NFL's Thanksgiving tripleheader produced three close finishes.

Published November 27, 2026 · By Public Opinion News Editorial Team

The NFL Thanksgiving games delivered three dramatic finishes on November 26, with Chicago, Philadelphia and Buffalo all winning through late scoring plays. The Bears beat the Detroit Lions 39-35 after trailing for most of the afternoon. The Eagles overturned a 21-point deficit to defeat the Dallas Cowboys 39-38, while the Bills edged the Kansas City Chiefs 13-10 on a field goal as time expired. Reuters and the Associated Press reported that all three contests had a major effect on the division standings as the regular season moved toward December.

Chicago rallies late to beat Detroit

The Chicago Bears defeated the Detroit Lions 39-35 after scoring late in the fourth quarter. Caleb Williams found Rome Odunze for a six-yard touchdown with three seconds remaining, giving Chicago its first lead of the game. The Bears had trailed 35-24 with just over eight minutes left, but the final stretch changed the result and moved Chicago ahead of Detroit in the NFC North.

Williams completed 30 of 44 passes for 345 yards and two touchdowns, according to Reuters. Odunze delivered the winning catch, while Jahmyr Gibbs produced 205 rushing yards and three touchdowns for Detroit in a losing effort. The NFL Thanksgiving games began with a contest that featured a high-scoring finish and a major divisional swing.

The NFL Thanksgiving games will be judged by the practical steps that follow, not by the language of the announcement alone.

Detroit's offense produced big plays but could not close

Gibbs gave the Lions a late 35-24 advantage with a 70-yard touchdown run. His performance kept Detroit in control for much of the afternoon and showed why the team remains dangerous when its running game is working. Jared Goff passed for 313 yards and a touchdown, but Chicago's defence created a late opportunity with an interception off a deflection.

Philadelphia completes a 21-point comeback

The Philadelphia Eagles beat the Dallas Cowboys 39-38 after trailing by 21 points late in the third quarter. The Eagles scored 22 points in the fourth period and took their first lead on Jake Elliott's 59-yard field goal with two seconds left. The comeback was one of the day's most striking moments and added pressure to a Dallas team that had been ahead by a wide margin.

Joe Milton III threw for 331 yards and three touchdowns, with one interception, Reuters reported. DeVonta Smith caught 11 passes for 200 yards and two scores. The Eagles' defence also made a crucial play by stopping Dak Prescott on fourth down, giving Philadelphia the ball for its final drive.

Dallas let a commanding lead slip away

The Cowboys had built a 31-10 advantage and briefly restored a two-score lead in the fourth quarter. Javonte Williams scored three first-half touchdowns, and Prescott added both passing and rushing scores. But Dallas could not prevent Philadelphia from producing explosive plays when the game entered its final minutes.

Buffalo wins a defensive battle against Kansas City

The Buffalo Bills beat the Kansas City Chiefs 13-10 in a game shaped by rain, defensive pressure and a late field-goal opportunity. Tyler Bass kicked a 57-yard field goal as time expired after Buffalo's return unit gave the offence enough field position to attempt the kick. The win moved the Bills to 10-1 and ended Kansas City's three-game winning streak.

James Cook rushed for 117 yards and a touchdown for Buffalo. Josh Allen completed only 10 of 21 passes for 81 yards and threw two interceptions. Patrick Mahomes finished with 174 passing yards and no touchdown pass. The NFL Thanksgiving games showed that a team can win a major matchup even when its passing offence is held below its usual level.

What the results mean for the division races

The NFL Thanksgiving games moved Chicago to 9-2 and put the Bears ahead of Detroit, which fell to 8-3. That matters because the NFC North has been one of the league's strongest divisions. A late-season division lead can shape playoff seeding, home-field advantage and the path through the postseason.

Quarterback performances told different stories

Williams delivered the most complete passing line of the day with 345 yards and two touchdowns. Milton produced 331 yards and three touchdown passes while leading Philadelphia's comeback. Allen's numbers were modest, but Buffalo still found a way to win through its ground game, defence and special teams.

Special teams became the difference at the end

Two of the three games ended with long field goals. Elliott's 59-yard kick gave Philadelphia the lead with two seconds remaining, while Bass hit from 57 yards as the clock expired in Buffalo. Both attempts required accurate execution under pressure, and both turned close contests into victories.

Why the Thanksgiving tripleheader draws attention

Thanksgiving is one of the NFL's most established calendar traditions. The league uses the holiday schedule to showcase rivalry games and give fans a full day of football. This year's results offered close finishes, high-scoring sequences and direct consequences for division races, making the slate especially compelling.

What comes next in Week 13

The NFL Thanksgiving games leave all three winners needing to carry their momentum into a short turnaround before the next round. Chicago will try to protect its division lead, Buffalo will aim to keep pressure on its AFC rivals, and Philadelphia will need more consistency if it wants to turn one dramatic win into a longer run.
